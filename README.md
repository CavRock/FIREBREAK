# FIREBREAK
This is the stone tablet of the high timber. It was not chiseled for those who sleep behind brick walls or trade coins in stone halls. It is the reckoning of thirty-five paces of earth, twenty-five paces of green pine, and the red hunger that walks between them when the drought breaks.
Here are carved the seventeen rituals of the timber-keeper. They govern how flame crawls through wood, how charcoal remembers heat, and how living pine stands against the wind.
The laws carved upon this slate are immutable:
Green timber is silent. It does not breed, it does not move, and it does not seek its brothers. It waits in the dirt until three flames surround it.
Flame is the only beast that breathes. Where three embers gather, new fire is born. Where flame starves alone, or smothers in its own smoke, it collapses into cold soot.
Ash is the permanent scar of the mountain. Wood that burns once is dead forever; it will never sprout green needles again in the lifetime of man. Yet the soot remains dry and hungry—if three roaring flames sweep back over dead charcoal, the dead earth rekindles into living fire.
The keeper of the wood holds one strike of flint for each watch. By striking flint against the ground, the keeper may smother a creeping ember into black soot, rekindle cold ash into a counter-blaze, or sacrifice a green tree to cut a barren line through the canopy. One mark upon the earth, and no more, before the wind stirs.
Ten ancestral groves are chiseled within this slate, from the first lonely spark in the low brush to the roaring inferno of the high peak. Beyond them lies the eleventh wilderness, where the bones are cast blindly into the dirt and wild fire hunts without pattern or master.
If every flame is starved while green pine remains standing, the canopy endures. If the red hunger devours all timber until only black ash covers the mountain, the forest is lost.
Read the marks. Count the living wood. Guard the forest.
